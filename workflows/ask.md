# Ask

A question can be about one paper ("How does Zep decide a fact is outdated?"), several ("Who decides what gets stored in MemGPT and in A-MEM?"), a topic ("Where do the benchmarks disagree?"), or my own work ("Has anyone tested memory updates the way I plan to?"). It can also be writing: a related work section, a comparison table, a reading list for a new labmate.

## Answer

1. Start from the wiki: the index, then the topic and paper pages that cover the question. Search the wiki for specific terms.
2. When the wiki isn't detailed enough, go back to the paper in `library/`. If what you find belongs on its paper page, add it there too.
3. Answer in a few sentences, linking the paper pages behind each point. Separate what papers show from what you infer.
4. If the wiki doesn't cover the question well, say so plainly, and suggest the papers that would fill the gap. Search arXiv if the reading list doesn't have them. Don't stretch thin evidence.

## Fold it back

A good answer shouldn't disappear into the chat. When one produces something lasting, such as a new comparison, a connection the pages missed, or a sharper "Where it stands", fold it into the right topic or paper page. When it's a new question that several papers speak to, make it a topic. Tell me in one line what you added.

Passing questions need none of this. While we're still going back and forth, wait until the discussion settles before writing anything.

## Writing

When I ask for writing to use outside the wiki, like a related work section, a survey outline, or notes for a talk, save it to `output/<short-name>.md` and link it in your reply.

* Write from the wiki, organized by idea, not paper by paper. The topic pages already have the structure.
* Cite papers as (First author et al., year) and list them at the end with their arXiv links.
* When it's for my own paper, place my work against the others: what it shares with them and where it differs.
* Before saving, check every claim against the paper page it comes from, and every number against the paper.
