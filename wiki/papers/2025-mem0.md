# Mem0: Building Production-Ready AI Agents with Scalable Long-Term Memory

Prateek Chhikara et al. (Mem0) · arXiv, Apr 2025 · [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413) · Read: full paper

**Topics:** [How memory is organized](../topics/memory-structures.md) · [Updating and forgetting](../topics/updating-and-forgetting.md) · [Evaluating memory](../topics/evaluating-memory.md) · [Memory vs. long context](../topics/memory-vs-long-context.md)

Mem0 extracts facts from each exchange and has an LLM decide, fact by fact, whether to add, update, delete, or skip it against similar stored memories; a graph variant stores entities and relations. On LoCoMo it beats the other memory systems it tests on an LLM-judge score, but full context still scores higher, with much higher latency. The authors work at Mem0, which sells the system.

## Problem

Context windows can't hold weeks or months of conversation, and putting the whole history in context is slow and expensive. Production agents need memory that is accurate, fast, and cheap.

## Method

* **Extraction:** from the latest exchange, a rolling summary of the conversation, and the last 10 messages, an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) extracts candidate facts.
* **Update:** for each candidate, retrieve the 10 most similar stored memories. The LLM picks one operation through a tool call: ADD (no equivalent memory exists), UPDATE (add complementary information to a memory), DELETE (the memory is contradicted by the new fact), or NOOP.
* **Mem0^g, the graph variant:** entities (with a type, an embedding, and a creation timestamp) and relation triplets in Neo4j. A conflict detector flags existing relations that conflict with new ones, and an LLM resolver marks obsolete relations invalid instead of deleting them. Retrieval combines entity-centric search and semantic triplet search.

## Results

LoCoMo, with the adversarial category excluded, scored with F1, BLEU-1, and an LLM judge (J):

* **Overall J:** full context 72.90, Mem0^g 68.44, Mem0 66.88, best RAG setup 60.97.
* **By category (J, Mem0's labels):** Mem0 leads single-hop (67.13) and multi-hop (51.15); Mem0^g leads temporal (58.13); Zep leads open-domain (76.60). OpenAI's memory scores 21.71 on temporal.
* **Latency, p95 total:** Mem0 1.440 s, Mem0^g 2.590 s, Zep 2.926 s, full context 17.117 s.
* **Memory size:** about 7K tokens per conversation for Mem0, 14K for Mem0^g, and over 600K for Zep's graph.
* **Headline claims:** 26% relative improvement in J over OpenAI's memory; 91% lower p95 latency and over 90% lower token cost than full context.

## Limits

* No limitations section. Full context beats both variants on overall J, and the graph variant trails base Mem0 on single-hop and multi-hop questions.
* The authors ran the Zep and OpenAI baselines themselves (Zep through its platform). The LoCoMo, ReadAgent, MemoryBank, MemGPT, and A-MEM rows are reprinted from A-MEM, so they have F1 and BLEU-1 but no J; only A-MEM was re-run.

## Ideas worth reusing

* **One update decision per fact:** For each extracted fact, the LLM picks ADD, UPDATE, DELETE, or NOOP against the 10 most similar memories, through a tool call.
* **Retire a fact instead of deleting it:** Mem0^g marks a contradicted relation invalid instead of deleting it. Also in [Zep](2025-zep.md).

## For my work

Mem0 makes the update decision explicit, and its two variants take opposite sides on what to do with a contradicted fact: base Mem0 deletes it, Mem0^g marks it invalid. But LoCoMo has no knowledge-update questions, so the paper doesn't show how well either choice handles changed facts.
